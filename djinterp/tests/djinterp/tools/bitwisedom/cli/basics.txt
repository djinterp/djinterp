$ bitwisedom --help
> Usage: bitwisedom [OPTION]... COMMAND [ARGUMENT]...
> A bitfield manager: sheets of named bits, written out as C or C++ macros.
>
> Reading a sheet:
>   gen                 write a sheet's header, in C or C++
>   check               report every issue of every sheet; write nothing else
>   show                list every row as it resolves: name, kind, bits and value
>   decode              say what values hold: each bit set, each field's value and
>                       its name
>   eval                evaluate C expressions over a sheet's symbols, every way
>   convert             write a sheet in another shape: a sheet file, a bitfield
>                       or an export
>   examples            list the built-in examples, by shelf
>
> Making and changing a sheet:
>   new                 write a new sheet, as File > New makes one
>   import              make a sheet from C: each #define a row, read as the
>                       preprocessor reads it
>   add                 add a row, defined as a bitfield's row is
>   change              change a row: its definition, or anything else the
>                       inspector shows
>   duplicate           copy rows, each just after itself, named as it is with
>                       _COPY after
>   remove              remove rows, and everything in them
>   rename              rename a row; every expression that names it follows
>   move                move a row: up, down, beside another, or into a group
>   sort                put each group's rows in order, for the header to follow
>   set                 change settings, by the paths the window's Options show
>   get                 print settings; every one, without a KEY
>   op                  preview an operation over rows -- a shift, a rotation, a
>                       byte swap, arithmetic; --apply applies it
>
> The window:
>   gui                 open the window on the sheets named, or where it was left
>
> Commands:
>   help                display help for a command
>   completion          print a completion script for a shell
>
> Options:
>   -q, --quiet         say nothing but errors
>   -v, --verbose       say more; twice, more again
>       --color[=WHEN]  colour diagnostics: auto (the default), always or never
>   -h, --help          display this help and exit
>       --version       output version information and exit
>
> Exit status:
>   0  if it did what was asked, and the answer, if one was asked, is yes
>   1  if it did, and the answer is no
>   2  if there was trouble: the command line, a file, or the program
$ bitwisedom help add
> Usage: bitwisedom add [OPTION]... SHEET NAME
> Add a row, defined as a bitfield's row is.
>
> Arguments:
>   SHEET                    the sheet, written back where it was read; - for
>                            standard input, written to standard output
>   NAME                     its own name, without the affixes around it
>
> Options:
>       --in=ROW             the group or range it goes in, last (default: the
>                            sheet's own rows)
>       --note=TEXT          its brief, in the header
>       --short=TEXT         the abbreviation the grid may show
>       --prefix=TEXT        what its name, and its rows', begin with
>       --suffix=TEXT        what its name, and its rows', end with
>       --colour=COLOUR      its colour: #RRGGBB, #RRGGBBAA, auto or none
>       --emit, --no-emit    put it in the header, or keep it out
>       --isolate, --no-isolate
>                            a group's or a range's: ignore the affixes of the
>                            groups around it, or not
>       --name-children, --no-name-children
>                            a group's or a range's: begin its rows' names with
>                            its own, or not
>       --exclusive, --no-exclusive
>                            a group's or a range's: warn when its rows' bits
>                            overlap, or not
>       --radix=RADIX        how its literal is written: inherit, hex, dec, oct,
>                            bin or shift
>       --int-suffix=SUFFIX  its literal's suffix: inherit, none, u, ul or ull
>       --digits=N           the fewest digits its literal is written with
>   -o, --out=FILE           write the sheet to FILE, - for standard output, and
>                            leave the one read as it was
>   -n, --dry-run            write nothing; say what would change
>   -f, --force              write it even if the change gives the sheet errors
>   -h, --help               display this help and exit
>
> Definitions:
>       --bit[=N]            a bit: bit N, or the next free
>       --bits=RANGE         a range: 4..7, 7..4, or one bit
>       --lsb[=N]            a range from bit N, or the next free
>       --width=N            the range's width, with --lsb (default: 1)
>       --field=ROW          a value in the range ROW
>       --number=N           the value's number (default: 0)
>       --encoding=ENCODING  how the number is held: unsigned, twos_complement,
>                            ones_complement, sign_magnitude or offset_binary
>                            (default: unsigned)
>       --bias=N             offset binary's bias (default: half the field's
>                            range)
>       --value=N            a literal
>       --expr=TEXT          an expression over the sheet's symbols
>       --expr-file=FILE     the expression, read from FILE
>       --body=TEXT          a macro's body
>       --body-file=FILE     the body, read from FILE
>       --params=TEXT        the macro's parameters: "x, v"
>       --callable, --no-callable
>                            a function-like macro, even with no parameters; or
>                            not
>       --group              a group
>
> Global options:
>   -q, --quiet              say nothing but errors
>   -v, --verbose            say more; twice, more again
>       --color[=WHEN]       colour diagnostics: auto (the default), always or
>                            never
